Research Areas

MagCIL works on multimodal machine learning for audio, speech, music, image and video. Four core areas carry most of our scientific output; each of them reaches practice through a small number of application domains, where the problem, the data and the constraints come from a real setting rather than a benchmark.

Core areas

Speech Analytics

We model both what people say and how they say it, with an emphasis on behaviour that survives real-world conditions: noise, channel shift, accent and language.

  • Speech emotion recognition and its robustness under distribution shift: benchmarking self-supervised encoders against acoustic corruption (ICASSP 2026), and the RobuSER benchmark (ACII 2024)
  • Paralinguistics: speaking style and public-speaking quality, filled-pause detection, engagement and speaker diarization
  • Clinical and wellbeing speech: cognitive decline and Alzheimer's detection, depression estimation, relapse detection from wearable-derived signals
  • Speech recognition for Greek and other low-resource settings, including domain-specific transcription
  • Expressive and controllable speech synthesis, and the detection of synthetic speech

Music Information Retrieval and Music Generation

A long-running line of work on musical content, increasingly focused on idioms that mainstream models represent poorly, Greek traditional, folk and laiko music among them.

  • Datasets for underrepresented repertoires, including Lyra for Greek traditional and folk music (ISMIR 2022), and ongoing work on modal and rhythmic annotation of Greek laiko
  • Recognition of genre, instrument, playing technique, regional style, mood and emotion
  • Audio fingerprinting for song identification and music-use monitoring, evaluated under real-world noise and signal processing
  • Text-to-audio and text-to-music generation, with current work on inference-time verification and scaling of diffusion and flow-matching models
  • Symbolic music generation with interpretable attribute control through latent-space disentanglement (EUSIPCO 2026)

Computer Vision and Image Generation

Representation learning and generative modelling for images and video, built on vision foundation models and self-supervised pretraining.

  • Latent diffusion and the entanglement of global and local semantics for controllable image generation (ECCV 2026)
  • Self-supervised and few-shot visual representation learning, and multi-task classification of visual attributes
  • Video understanding: summarization, classification and retrieval, shot-type and style analysis
  • Multimodal fusion of sound, image and text for content characterization and recommendation
  • Human activity, gesture and intention recognition, including real-time human–robot settings

Audio Event Recognition and Acoustic Scene Analysis

Non-speech, non-music audio: what a recording tells you about the place it was made in and the events that happened there.

  • Sound event detection and acoustic scene classification, with open datasets such as ARCHEO and the Athens Urban Soundscape (ATHUS)
  • Urban soundscape monitoring at scale, from crowdsourced recordings to a spatiotemporal map
  • Bioacoustics, including unsupervised analysis of mouse vocal communication with the Jarvis Lab at Rockefeller University
  • Few-shot and prototypical learning for acoustic classes with very little labelled data (MLSP 2025)
  • Adversarial robustness of state-of-the-art audio transformers against black-box attacks (EUSIPCO 2025)

Application domains

Culture and Cultural Heritage

AI for Greek musical and linguistic heritage: semantic infrastructure for traditional and laiko music, automatic transcription and indexing of archival speech, emotion in theatrical performance, and the urban soundscape treated as living heritage. Carried out largely within the Language & Culture vertical of the PHAROS AI Factory, with the Ministry of Culture among the interested parties.

Health and Wellbeing

Speech and physiological signals as low-cost markers of health: cognitive decline and dementia, depression, mental-health relapse, and unobtrusive monitoring in assisted-living settings. The same work underpins accessibility applications such as real-time subtitling for people with hearing loss.

Defence and Security

Frugal and robust AI for settings where data is scarce, compute is constrained and models are adversarially probed: few-shot acoustic recognition, adversarial robustness evaluation, AI-assisted annotation pipelines, and paralinguistic triage at borders.

Environment and Sustainability

Acoustic and remote sensing for environmental monitoring: urban soundscape quality, transport-related air pollution, marine traffic and environmental risk in the Aegean, and the attribution of chemical pollution sources from screening data.

Trustworthy and Synthetic Media

As generative audio and video become routine, so does the need to tell them apart from the real thing. We work on synthetic-media detection and on public-facing AI literacy, including a national deepfake awareness platform funded by the Ministry of Digital Governance.

AI Infrastructure and Open Science

Making the above usable by others: open-source libraries such as pyAudioAnalysis, deepaudio-x and deepaudio-lab, released datasets and benchmarks, and contributions to national and European AI platforms through PHAROS and DeployAI.

Methodological foundations

Running underneath all four areas is a shared methodological agenda, which is also where our most general results sit: self-supervised and few-shot representation learning, the theory and objectives of contrastive learning (ICML 2024, ICML 2026, NeurIPS workshops), generative modelling with diffusion and flow matching, robustness and calibration under distribution shift, and multimodal fusion. Methods developed for one modality routinely move to another, which is the main reason the group keeps all four areas under one roof.
